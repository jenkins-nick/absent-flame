# ELEUSINA — A GAZETTEER

*The world of **The Absent Flame**. Companion to `absent-flame-world-map.svg`. Place names borrowed from Destiny 1 and 2 and re-pointed at this campaign's geography.*

The world is **Eleusina**, and the map is **player-safe**. No rider is named on it, nothing gives away Kavzar, and every phenomenon marked on it is something a survivor could have reported. Hand it out after chapter 1, part E, when the rumours arrive.

---

## THE PLATE AT A GLANCE

The map is **unnumbered on purpose**. Twilight Gap is where the campaign opens and the Pale Heart is where it has to end; everything between is the party's to sequence, and whatever they leave for last has grown by the time they get there.

| Place | Sword | Arc | Chapter | Levels if run in the recommended order |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Twilight Gap** | the Ember | prologue | 1. The Last Battle | 1–2 |
| **Felwinter Peak** | the Dawn | the Hollow King | 2. The Waking Peak | 2–4 |
| **Hellmouth** | the Vigil | the Hollow King | 3. The Sunless Deep | 4–6 |
| **Dust Palace** | the Feast | the Gaunt Rider | 4. The Groaning Table | 6–8 |
| **Six Fronts** | the Truce | the War-Mother | 5. The Unfinished Field | 8–10 |
| **Kavzar** | the Hush | the Kind Lie | 6. The Silent City | 10–12 |
| **Harbinger's Seclude** | the Solitude | the Kind Lie | 7. The Anchorhold | 12–14 |
| **The Pale Heart** | — | finale | 8. The Rekindling | 14–15 |

**Felwinter Peak and the Hellmouth travel together** (one arc, two sites), as do **Kavzar and Harbinger's Seclude**. So the party is really choosing an order for four things, not seven — and the Kind Lie pair goes last regardless. Scale encounters per "Arc Order" if a pair is taken well out of its band.

Seven marks, seven swords, and the eighth is the hole in the middle of the world where they all have to go.

## THE SITES

### Twilight Gap — *the border pass*
A saddle between the western hills and the Rusted Lands, and the reason two armies were standing in the same mud at dusk. The war was a border war and this was the border. By noon the next day it was over as an organized activity.

The Ember is still in the field somewhere, unless a character has it on their hip. **Firebase Rubicon**, a crater-scarred stretch southeast, and **Widow's Court**, a ruined manor town just north, are where survivors scattered to — good sources for the evacuee NPCs the campaign will keep asking you for.

### Felwinter Peak — *the last lit place*
The high wild's tallest spire, at the western end of the **Endless Steps**. The Dawn came down into the sky-shard at its summit and has been burning without heat ever since.

- **The Lantern Gate** — the wagon-wreck wall, chapter 2 area 3.
- **Archer's Line** — the switchbacks. Exposure, thinning air, and the Gloaming always one turn below.
- **Anchor of Light** — Warden Ivet Callow's refuge, three thousand people in concentric rings around a light nobody owns. (This is Hearthold. Use whichever name your table likes; "Anchor of Light" is what the refugees call it and it's a better thing for them to shout at each other.)
- **Skywatch** — an older ruin above the refuge, where the ascent's first watchers are posted. Useful for a warning, a body, or a two-shadowed sentry.

### Hellmouth — *the wound in the deep country*
A shaft punched clean through the world's crust, half a mile across at the lip. It is the only site on this map that is mostly *under* it.

- **Sorrow's Harbor** — the Hanging Town, abandoned in good order, doors closed, tools racked.
- **The Asterion Abyss** — where the Gloaming pools like water. Light or high ground; nothing else.
- **Circle of Bones** — a mile of stairs walled in stacked bone. Osteomancy works extraordinarily well here.
- **The Chamber of Night** — the necropolis floor, and the single white point of the Vigil very far away in absolute dark.

### Dust Palace — *the buried god's table*
Under the **Steppes**, the world's breadbasket, now under permanent frost. The Feast tore **Giant's Scar** through the fields on its way down; walk the trench and it ends in dark.

Nearby: the **Mothyards** (a dead orchard the Almoner's flock still harvests), **Fields of Glass** and the **Infinite Forest** to the northwest (see the Weft, below), **Charon's Crossing**, **Scablands**, **Lost Oasis**, and the **Valley of the Kings** running south — dry country that was marginal before the frost and is uninhabitable now.

### Six Fronts — *the battle that never finished*
A legendary field on the southern border. Named for the six armies that met here; nobody agrees which one lost, which is part of why it never stopped. The Truce stands planted at the center like a banner nobody has been able to reach.

- **Bannerfall** — the broken fortress at the field's edge, and the only shelter within a night's walk.
- **Firebase Delphi** — Marshal Oren Vasque's camp, eleven thousand refugees under one banner.
- **Unfinished Field** — the field proper. Empty and crossable by day. Not by night.

### Kavzar — *the Silent City*
Marked on the map exactly the way the world believes it: a dead capital, an old story, nobody goes. The **Divalian Mists** hang in the outer rings. The **Spine of Keres** is the ridge road in from the southeast. The tower is drawn leaning, because it is.

What the map does not show is the population. See "What the map is lying about."

### Harbinger's Seclude — *the last lonely thing*
A rock in the **Ecliptic Drift**, off the eastern coast past **Liming Harbor**. One cell, one occupant, one sword, and an approach the last mile of which can only be walked by one person at a time.

Liming Harbor is the only port that will still take a charter out there, and the harbourmaster will want to know why.

### The Pale Heart — *the dead fire* · NOT MARKED
Nothing is drawn here but the caldera itself. Dead center of the southern **Hellas Basin**: a caldera ringed by scarps, drawn on the map as the hachured depression it is. **Cadmus Ridge** is the descent. At the bottom is a hearth the size of a city square with nothing in it.

Cartographers have drawn this basin for an age without ever marking anything at the bottom of it, because there was never anything to mark. Nobody has ever needed to go. Your players' copy is drawn exactly the same way, and that is the joke: the hole has been sitting in the middle of their map since the first session.

## THE REST OF THE MAP

**Seas.** The **Ocean of Storms** to the north, the **Ecliptic Drift** east, the **Shores of Time** and the **Sunken Isles** south. Sea travel is the fastest way anywhere and nobody wants to do it in the dark.

**The heartland.** **Trostland** and **Sanctuary** sit at the road junction in the middle of everything, which makes them the campaign's default hub: where refugee columns arrive, where rumours are traded, where the party's growing crowd of rescued NPCs ends up living. The **Rusted Lands** around them are old warfields, ruin-scattered and picked over.

**The Weft's country.** The **Infinite Forest** in the northwest — not a forest, a repeating one. Trees in the same arrangement, over and over, for miles, and **Fields of Glass** at the edge where the pattern was still being worked out. Seed this from chapter 2: a party that has walked past a too-perfect copy of something four times before chapter 6 will take the Weft seriously when they matter.

**Gloaming lowlands.** The **Miasma**, the **Weeping Well**, and the **Dark Forest** in the southeast, plus everything at the map's edges. These are the places already lost. Use them for the refugee columns coming *toward* the party.

**Winding Cove.** The last inhabited waypoint on the road down to the Hellmouth. Whoever the characters leave here is who they come back to.

## TRAVEL

Roads are dotted on the map; the sea route to Harbinger's Seclude is dashed. On foot, in the new light, roughly:

| From | To | Days |
|---|---|---|
| Twilight Gap | Trostland | 12 |
| Trostland | Kavzar | 15 |
| Trostland | Six Fronts | 21 |
| Twilight Gap | The Dust Palace | 18 |
| Kavzar | Felwinter Peak | 18 |
| Kavzar | The Hellmouth | 29 |
| Kavzar | Liming Harbor | 25 |
| Liming Harbor | Harbinger's Seclude | 10 (by sea) |
| Six Fronts | The Pale Heart | 22 |
| The Hellmouth | The Pale Heart | 17 |

These are deliberately long. The adventure assumes a year to eighteen months and real downtime between arcs; the map should make the party feel the size of the thing they are crossing. Add a third to anything that crosses the frost line or a Gloaming lowland.

## THE FOUR OVERLAYS

Everything marked in colour is something the characters can see, hear, or feel. Redraw them between arcs — a map that changes is the only weather report this world has.

**Only two of these are drawn on the plate** — the frost line and the fever. The Gloaming, the toll, and the Pale Heart were cut so the map gives away nothing a party has not earned. Add them by hand as the characters learn them, or keep the map clean and let the table find out the hard way.

| Overlay | What it is | How it moves |
|---|---|---|
| **Frost line** (dashed pale blue, hatched on the advancing side) | The famine front | Advances out of the northwest whether or not anyone fights it. Stops and recedes when chapter 4's Avatar goes down. |
| **Violet stain** (map edges, and one tongue reaching up from the south) | The Gloaming | Creeps inward from every edge. Thins in a one-mile circle wherever the Dawn is drawn. Recedes when chapter 3's Avatar goes down. |
| **Concentric rings** (out of Kavzar) | The toll | *Not drawn.* Add rings by hand if you want the toll visible; how far it reaches, and what it takes when it gets there, is a question the adventure deliberately leaves to you. |
| **Crossed blades** | The fever | Cluster them wherever the party has recently fought. Clear them from the borderlands when chapter 5's Avatar goes down. |

If you do draw the Gloaming in, have it **part around Kavzar** without explaining why. Let a player notice. It is the single best free clue you can hand them, and it costs nothing.

## WHAT THE MAP IS LYING ABOUT

*DM only.*

1. **Kavzar is drawn as a ruin.** It is fully populated and it is one person. The map is wrong in exactly the way every map in the world is wrong, and that is the point — when the characters work it out in chapter 6, the map they have been navigating by becomes evidence that nobody ever checked.
2. **The tower is drawn as architecture.** It is a vessel. It has no foundation. It came down before the city and the city was built around it.
3. **The Pale Heart has no road to it.** Correct, and damning. An age of scripture describes the Flame's augmentation as a rescue and not one cartographer ever thought the site was worth a track.
4. **The frost line, the stain, the rings, and the blades are drawn as four separate weather systems.** By the end of chapter 1 the characters can name four things going wrong. They have no idea they are looking at four people. Keep the overlays visually unrelated for as long as you can.
5. **Harbinger's Seclude is barely on the map at all** — a rock, no port, no reason. She has been out there since Stage Two, and the only being in the world who cannot go and look is the one who most needs to.

## NAMES HELD IN RESERVE

For the sites you improvise. All Destiny-flavoured, none used above.

**House rule:** no place on this map is called *The Something*. Labels carry the bare name — Hellmouth, Mothyards, Steppes — and the article only shows up in a sentence, where grammar needs it. If a bare name sounds too thin to be a place, that is the signal to pick a different name rather than prop it up with an article.
